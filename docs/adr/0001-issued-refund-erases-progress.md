# An Issued Refund erases the learner's Progress for good

When a Refund is issued, the learner's Progress in that course is truly erased, not hidden or kept for a possible return. We chose this because a Refund should carry a real consequence: the learner is warned before raising the Refund Request, and if they buy the course again they start from zero.

## The decisions

1. **Raising a Refund Request locks the course.** The course is Locked from the moment the request is Open. Progress is untouched.
2. **The learner is warned first.** Before submitting, they confirm a warning that says: they lose access during review, their Progress is erased if the Refund is paid, and a future purchase of the course can't be refunded.
3. **A learner can withdraw an Open Refund Request.** The course unlocks at once with Progress as it was. There is no withdrawing after approval.
4. **A Declined Refund Request unlocks the course** with Progress as it was. The cost of asking was the time locked out.
5. **Approval alone erases nothing.** After approval the course stays Locked and Progress stays untouched until the Refund is issued, so a learner never loses their Progress before their money is back.
6. **An Issued Refund ends access and erases Progress.** A team member marks the Refund as issued, behind a confirmation that it can't be undone. That one action does both.
7. **Progress means everything in that course:** lessons started and completed, quiz attempts, watch history, the learner's rating, and their comments. Comments show as deleted so other learners' replies stay readable. The learner's other courses are untouched.
8. **The erase is a true erase.** Support can't restore it.
9. **A refunded learner can buy the course again**, starting from zero.
10. **One Refund per learner per course, ever.** A purchase made after an Issued Refund is a Final Purchase and can never be refunded.
11. **A learner can raise a new Refund Request** after a withdrawal or a decline, under the usual limits on when a purchase is refundable.

## Scope

These decisions cover the Refund Request from raise to decision, the Refund reaching issued, and what happens to access and Progress along the way. The movement of money is outside them: how it is sent, retried, or handled when it can't be sent.

## Considered Options

- **Keep Progress** so a returning learner resumes where they left off. Rejected: it makes a Refund free of consequence. The harm to an honest learner who returns later was weighed and accepted.
- **Hide Progress** behind a flag so support could restore it. Rejected: it keeps refunded learners in the data, and every instructor report would have to filter them out.
- **Erase at approval.** Rejected: the learner could lose their Progress before their money is back.
- **Lock only at approval.** Rejected: a learner could keep watching a course they have asked to be paid back for.

## Consequences

- Erasing watch history resets the limits that make a purchase refundable, which would let a learner buy, watch, refund and repeat. Decision 10 exists to close that loop.
- A refunded learner disappears from the instructor's roster, drop-off funnel, quiz pass rates and course rating as if they had never enrolled.
